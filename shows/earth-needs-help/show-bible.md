# Earth Needs Help — Show Bible

## Core premise
A tiny alien rescue crew receives what they believe is an emergency signal from Earth. They arrive expecting an intergalactic catastrophe, but ordinary human problems look like planet-level emergencies to them. Each episode turns a small Earth problem into a funny rescue mission.

## Audience and tone
- Target: roughly ages 5–9.
- Tone: warm, funny, adventurous, safe, energetic, never babyish.
- Comedy: visual reactions, misunderstanding Earth customs, overcomplicated alien solutions, then a simple human or emotional truth.
- Episode takeaway: light and natural; never preachy.

## Visual language
Original polished 3D-style cartoon look. Soft rounded forms, expressive readable eyes, colourful environments, tactile materials, gentle cinematic lighting, simple silhouettes and strong character colour coding. Characters must remain easy to reproduce consistently across many generated shots.

## Main cast
### Bloop
Small round turquoise-blue alien with two short flexible antennae, large oval eyes, tiny limbs and a squidgy body. Enthusiastic, curious, innocent and emotionally transparent. Often the first to get excited.

### Zig
Slim lime-green alien inventor with three head tufts/antennae, utility goggles and a small gadget belt. Clever, fast-moving and overconfident. Usually invents a technically impressive solution that creates more chaos before helping.

### Momo
Large fluffy lavender alien with enormous arms, tiny friendly eyes and a warm smile. Looks imposing but is extremely gentle. Often solves a problem simply because of size, patience or kindness.

### Captain Pip
Smallest alien, coral-red with an oversized dark-blue space captain jacket and original rescue insignia. Intensely serious. Treats tiny Earth inconveniences as intergalactic emergencies. Comedy comes from the mismatch between the problem and Pip's seriousness.

### Human child
Friendly, adventurous Earth kid around eight years old, practical clothes, colourful trainers and a small backpack. Patiently explains Earth life to the aliens and becomes their unofficial Earth expert.

## Recurring rules
1. No alien is cruel or threatening.
2. Earth problems remain child-safe and understandable.
3. The aliens can make a mess, but not cause frightening or dangerous harm.
4. Every episode should contain at least one visual gag that works without dialogue.
5. Each character should behave consistently with the traits above.
6. Avoid long exposition. Prefer action, reaction and short dialogue.
7. Keep recurring locations and props visually consistent through reference images.

## Episode format
Preferred first-run format: 60–120 seconds, 8–12 shots, 4–8 seconds per generated shot. Use a strong still reference for every shot, then animate from that still. Longer episodes can later be assembled from the same structure.
